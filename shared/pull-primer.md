# PULL in brief

> The PULL framework is the work of **Rob Snyder** (The Physics of Startups; Harvard Innovation Labs).
> This file is our own paraphrase for use inside the tool. Read the original:
> - https://thephysicsofstartups.substack.com/p/the-pull-framework
> - https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/an-86-minute-guide-to-finding-pmf

## The core idea

You cannot create demand. You can only find people who already have it and fit your product to them.
A buyer has PULL when four things are true at the same time:

| Letter | Means | Plain-English test |
|---|---|---|
| **P – Project** | They have a specific project on their own to-do list. | "Is there something they already intend to get done?" (Not a pain in general — a thing they plan to do.) |
| **U – Unavoidable** | It has to happen now. | "What happens if they don't do it this quarter?" There's a deadline, mandate, or consequence. |
| **L – Looking** | They're actively looking at ways to get it done. | Evaluating vendors, hiring for it, building it in-house, asking peers, hacking spreadsheets. |
| **L – Lacking** | The options they've found fall short for a reason that matters. | "Why haven't they just solved it with what exists?" |

When all four are true, the buyer pulls the product out of your hands. When any is missing, you are pushing.

## What this means for the tool

1. **An ICP is a Project, not a demographic.** The segment is "people who currently have Project X".
   Company size, industry and job title are only *where to look* for those people.
2. **Outreach tests, it doesn't persuade.** The best first message asks a question the buyer can answer
   yes or no: *is this project on your plate right now?* A "no" is useful data about the hypothesis.
3. **Pitch the project, not the product.** The buyer cares about getting their project done. The product
   is one line at most.
4. **The strongest demand is when alternatives literally cannot do the job.** We score Lacking in tiers
   to capture this (see `scoring-rubric.md`).
5. **PULL only counts if your product fits it.** A prospect with real PULL for a project you don't solve is
   not a lead.

## Common mistakes the tool guards against

- Treating a **pain** ("onboarding is slow") as a **project** ("we're rebuilding onboarding in Q1").
- **Invented urgency** — the seller supplying the "why now" instead of finding the buyer's own.
- **Feature-led messages** that talk about the product rather than the buyer's project.
- Mistaking **curiosity** (they read a blog post) for **Looking** (they're evaluating options).
- Writing to the wrong person: the user who feels the pain is not always the person who decides.
